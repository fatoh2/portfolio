## Overview

This replaces the homepage of fhaj.dev (`/`, `/ar`, `/he`) with the design drawn in
[preview H](../concepts/2026-11/previews/h-scope.html). Preview H combines concepts A, B, C, D and F. Those
concepts are defined in [REVIEW-2026-11.md](REVIEW-2026-11.md) section 4 and developed in sections 9 to 11:

*   A, "Follow one message": a visitor's message traced from channel to answer.
*   B, "The thread": the page as a question-and-answer thread.
*   C, "The spine": one sheet that turns between English, Arabic and Hebrew.
*   D, "The opening": the zoom through the ف of the monogram.
*   F, "The table": the projects dealt as cards on a card table.

The brainstorm that led here, with its six reviewers' findings, is [concepts_brief.md](../../concepts_brief.md).
This doc cites those findings as "finding B1–B7", "finding M1–M12" and "finding N1–N12". The owner's
decisions are cited as "choice A4", "choice B1" and so on (see "Owner choices"). Alternatives are cited as
"Alt-A" to "Alt-H".

The page has five parts:

1.  **The fold** (the first screen) is a folded sheet of paper lying on a card table. Its main leaf carries:
    *   the owner's name and role;
    *   the headline "I build what answers your customers." in the page's language;
    *   the résumé and WhatsApp buttons;
    *   the question box.

    The other leaf shows the same headline in the other two languages.
2.  **The question box** answers from rules written over the site's own twelve projects. The main path is
    suggested questions in each language ("chips"). A question the rules cannot answer gets an offer to send it
    on WhatsApp, prefilled with the visitor's words.
3.  **The zoom through the ف** reuses the zoom the site already has (described under "The zoom through the ف").
    Tapping "See it" under an answer jumps to it with that answer's project in view. Scrolling reaches it too.
4.  **The work** is twelve cards in the Mix look: the project's name set large over its phone screenshot. When
    the table first comes into view, the cards are dealt face down and land face up. Each card opens a sheet
    over the homepage that turns between English, Arabic and Hebrew. While the sheet is open, the address bar
    shows the project's case-page address (choice A4).
5.  **Services, process and contact** stay as visible text: services and process as a question-and-answer
    thread, then the contact form, every contact link and the footer.

The site header, the case pages and the three locale addresses stay as they are. So does every link,
download and linked section id on today's homepage, apart from the removals the owner signed (Non-Goals).

| Term | Meaning |

## The Problem

The current homepage uses the common portfolio layout: a hero, a filtered project deck, a services list, a
process list and a contact block. The owner's goal for this work, in the owner's words, is "to be more
creative about the design and make it look outside the box and the normal site created by everyone."

Two audiences read the page, and the design has to persuade both:

*   **Hirers**, who want the résumé, the stack and proof of shipped work fast.
*   **People looking for someone to build a project**, who want to see what has been built, what it does for a
    business, and how to start a conversation.

The six-reviewer brainstorm found that the first prototype, preview G, had dropped:

*   every APK download;
*   six case-page links;
*   Saqroon's site and download;
*   email, GitHub and LinkedIn;
*   the contact form;
*   the playable SOLitaire table.

Preview G also claimed to pass questions on when nothing was sent, and showed nothing without script.
Preview H addresses each of these in mock-up. This plan carries those fixes, and preview H's layout and
motion, into the Next.js build.

## Goals

*   **A distinct first screen.** The fold, the trilingual headline and the question box are on the first
    screen of `/`, `/ar` and `/he`. The owner judges the result with the swap test: no visitor sentence on the
    homepage would still be true if another developer's name replaced the owner's. Exempt from the swap test:
    navigation labels, button verbs, form labels and errors, project names, and the footer's legal lines.
*   **Résumé and WhatsApp on the first screen** in all three locales, at 360×640 and larger, without scrolling.
*   **Nothing lost.** Every link and download on today's homepage, and every section id that something links
    to, is still on the new page, visible and reachable. The exceptions are the removals the owner signed
    (Non-Goals). The APKs are no harder to reach: each card shows its downloads, so every APK is one tap
    from the work table. One vitest checks this in three locales. "Nothing lost" wins over the swap test when
    they conflict.
*   **Readable without script.** Every card, every answer the question box can give, and the services and
    process text are in the server HTML. Script adds the deal, the turn, the sheet and the zoom.
*   **Honest answers.** The question box never says something happened that did not. It answers from tested
    rules in English, Arabic and Hebrew, always in the page's language. Its unknown answer is an offer the
    visitor acts on.
*   **Measured use.** Today's event names stay. Eight new events show how the new parts are used: `ask`,
    `zoom_play`, `sheet_open`, `sheet_language`, `deal`, `demo_send`, `play_view` and `island_error`. None
    carries text a visitor typed.
*   **Works on a phone.** Every control on the fold and in the sheet can be reached with one thumb at 360 wide.
    It also works with reduced motion and with a screen reader. Android Back closes an open sheet instead of
    leaving the site.
*   **Rollback is one commit.** The new page is built as new files and switched in by one commit (see "Rollback"
    under Non-Functional Requirements for what that commit does not undo).

## Non-Goals

*   No AI model behind the question box. It costs money per visitor and could invent a price.
*   No server send from the question box. The visitor's question reaches the owner only if the visitor taps
    WhatsApp.
*   No edits to `src/app/[locale]/work/[slug]/page.tsx`, `src/components/project-page.tsx`, the site header
    or the locale addresses.
    *   Exception (choice D2, owner, 2026-11-11): the Latin font changes sitewide from Space Grotesk to
        Schibsted Grotesk in the cut-over commit, with a visual check of every case page and the header.
*   No case page for Saqroon. Its card carries its own site link, downloads and note.
*   No automatic scrolling. The page moves only when the visitor scrolls or taps.
*   No port of preview H's code. The canvas, `scope-data.js`, the hand-written rules and the inline CSS are
    reference only.
*   No new dependencies.
*   No invented statistics, customers or proof. No Instagram work shown. No paid image or video generation.
*   Not launching on 2026-11-11. Launch follows the device gate and the owner's read of the Arabic and Hebrew
    copy.
*   Removed from the homepage on purpose (owner, 2026-11-11):
    *   the five work filters, replaced by the calls;
    *   the per-project evidence lists, which stay on every case page.
*   Also removed on purpose (owner, 2026-11-15, E2): the install strip. Every APK stays one tap from the work
    table, in the download row on its card.
*   Owner decisions of 2026-11-15 on the review's questions:
    *   **E1:** the "Now" phase lines stay, moved from their own list to a line under each card's title (8 of
        12 cards).
    *   **E2:** see the removal above.
    *   **E3:** nothing outside the site links to `#agent`, so that id is dropped. `#saqroon` stays, because

## Open Questions

*   **Web Analytics is off for this project (owner, found 2026-11-15 in step 0.3).** The team is on the Pro
    plan, which allows custom events. But the `portfolio` project has never had Web Analytics enabled, so
    none of today's events, or the new ones, are being recorded:
    *   its project record has no `enabledAt`, while elsewhereco, seeker-launch-radar and go-to-nature do;
    *   the analytics query API answers "Web Analytics is not enabled for this project".

    Speed Insights is on and receiving data. Turning Web Analytics on is a project setting with a cost:
    $0.03 per 1,000 events on Pro, counting page views and custom events. Until the owner decides, the goal
    "Measured use" has no data behind it.
*   **The owner's read of the Arabic and Hebrew copy.** This gates the launch, not the plan.

**Settled in step 0 (2026-11-15; details in [REVIEW-2026-11.md](REVIEW-2026-11.md) section 12):**

*   **A4's history behaviour holds.** Spike 0.2 passed 51 of 51 checks on `/`, `/ar` and `/he` against a
    production build, covering:
    *   the address changes while the homepage stays rendered;
    *   Back, Forward and Escape;
    *   refresh opens the case page;
    *   the scroll position is kept;
    *   the header links are unchanged;
    *   the case page's language switch is not intercepted.

    Android Back on a real phone is left for the device gate. Vercel Analytics does queue a pageview for the
    case-page address when the sheet opens (see Risks).
*   **Vercel limits.** The Pro plan keeps **2 data fields per custom event** (8 with the Web Analytics Plus
    add-on, which this team does not have). Every new event therefore sends at most two fields, and
    `locale` is dropped first, as planned: `ask` sends `intent` and `source`. Three of today's events already
    send three fields (`slug`, `locale`, `surface`), so the third field of each is not kept.

## Critical User Journeys (CUJs)

### CUJ 1: A hirer gets the résumé

*   **Given**: A recruiter opens `https://fhaj.dev` on a phone at 375×667.
*   **When**: The page loads.
*   **Then**: The name, role, headline and a résumé button are on the first screen. One tap downloads the PDF
    and records `resume_download` with `placement: first-screen`.

### CUJ 2: A business owner asks what is live, then sees a project

*   **Given**: A visitor on `/ar` who wants to see shipped products.
*   **When**: They tap the suggested question for live products.
*   **Then**: The live-products answer replaces the chips. It is in Arabic and lists the live projects as links,
    with "See it" and "Ask another" beneath. Focus moves to the answer's heading, and the page does not scroll.
    `ask` is recorded with the matched intent and `source: chip` (two fields, the Pro plan's limit).
*   **When**: They tap "See it".
*   **Then**: The page jumps to the zoom with the first listed project's screenshot in the scene. `zoom_play`
    with `trigger: tap` fires once the opening is half open as they scroll. With reduced motion, or on a short
    screen, it fires on arrival.

### CUJ 3: A visitor asks something the rules cannot answer

*   **Given**: A visitor types "Can you build a booking system for my clinic?" in English.
*   **When**: They send it.
*   **Then**: The answer says the page cannot answer that and offers "Send it on WhatsApp". That link opens
    WhatsApp with the visitor's own question prefilled. Nothing is sent until they send it in WhatsApp.
*   **Events**: `ask` records `intent: unknown`. A tap on the link records `whatsapp_click` with
    `placement: answer`. The typed text appears in no event.

### CUJ 4: A visitor opens a card and goes back

*   **Given**: A visitor on Android Chrome scrolls to the work.
*   **When**: They tap the SOLitaire card.
*   **Then**: The sheet opens over the homepage in the page's language, with the live website and "Play a
    hand". The address bar shows SOLitaire's case-page address, and the page behind does not scroll.
*   **When**: They turn the sheet to Hebrew.
*   **Then**: The sheet's text turns to Hebrew in place, right to left.
*   **When**: They press the phone's Back button.
*   **Then**: The sheet closes, the live frames stop, and focus returns to the SOLitaire card. The address is
    the homepage again, at the same scroll position. Copying the case-page address while the sheet was open and
    opening it elsewhere opens the full case page.
*   **Events**: `sheet_open`, `sheet_language` and `play_view` are recorded.

### CUJ 5: A visitor installs a preview build

*   **Given**: A visitor wants to try an Android build.
*   **When**: They tap "Install a preview" above the cards, then a download link on a card.
*   **Then**: The cards with builds are dealt. Each shows its download links on its face. The APK downloads
    and records `apk_download` with `surface: card`.

### CUJ 6: A visitor without script, or a search engine

*   **Given**: Script is off, or a crawler fetches `/he`.
*   **When**: The page is read.
*   **Then**: All of the following are present in the HTML, in Hebrew, with `dir="rtl"`:
    *   the headline, and the résumé and WhatsApp links;
    *   all twelve cards, each with a link to its case page (Saqroon's links to its site) and its visible
        download links;
    *   every answer the question box can give;
    *   the services, process and contact sections.

### CUJ 7: A visitor follows a header link from a case page

*   **Given**: A visitor is on `/work/bab`.
*   **When**: They tap "Services", "Work", "Process" or "Contact" in the header.
*   **Then**: The homepage opens at the section with that id: `#services`, `#work`, `#process` or `#contact`.

## Technical Plan (Architecture)

### What changes and what stays

The new page is a new set of files under `src/components/home/`. The cut-over is one commit in which:

*   `src/app/[locale]/page.tsx` renders `HomeSheetPage` in place of `PortfolioHome`;
*   `src/app/[locale]/layout.tsx` swaps the Latin font.

No route files are added or moved. `PortfolioHome`, `ProjectDeck`, `WorkFilters` and their CSS stay in the
tree, unused, until a later dated commit deletes them.

| Kept as is | Reused with a change | New |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SiteHeader`, `ContactForm`, `TrackedLink` / `TrackedAnchor`, `LiveEmbed` (gains `onReady`), `DeckVideo`, `PortalMotion`, case pages, routes, locale routing | `agent-demo.ts` (export `normalize`), `agent-demo.tsx` (`demo_send`), `portfolio-home.tsx` (footer moved out into `SiteFooter`, used by both pages), `project-showcase.ts` (three `mobileIndex` entries), `ui.ts` (`form.failed`) | card builder, ask rules, fold, ask box, work table, project sheet, thread, home portal, island boundary, stylesheet, tests |

### Where the data comes from

Nothing is copied from the preview. Every card and answer is built on the server from the content files that
already exist:

```text
src/content/portfolio.ts ────────┐ projects (11), focusQuest (Saqroon), profile, services, statusLabels
src/content/project-showcase.ts ─┤ loops, phone screenshot index
src/content/now.json ────────────┤ phase lines (English, 8 of 12), one under each card's title
src/content/home.ts (new) ───────┘ headline ×3, chips, rules, answers, aliases, glossary, card order, labels
            │
            ▼
src/lib/home-cards.ts   buildCards(locale) → HomeCard[]                  (server only, pure)
src/lib/home-ask.ts     ask(text, matcher) → intent id                   (pure; the browser gets only the matcher)
                        answersFor(locale) → Answer[]                    (server only)
            │
            ▼
src/app/[locale]/page.tsx → HomeSheetPage (server): every card, every answer and the thread as HTML
   ├── Fold (server) ── AskBox (client island: reveals server-rendered answers)
   ├── HomePortal (server markup) ── PortalMotion (client, existing) + PortalPicture (client island)
   ├── #work: HomeCard ×12 (server) ── WorkTable (client island: deal, calls, owns ProjectSheet)
   ├── Thread (server): #services, #process, every answer (#q-…)
   └── #contact: ContactForm, contact links ── SiteFooter

src/app/[locale]/work/[slug]/page.tsx ── the case page, unchanged; what a shared or refreshed address opens
```

`HomeCard` is the one type that both content shapes map to: the eleven `projects` entries and `focusQuest`
(finding B7). Saqroon is the `focusQuest` entry. It has no case page, and it carries builds and a website
instead of `links`.

```text
HomeCard {
  slug, title, status,
  category: LocalizedText, summary: LocalizedText, statusNote: LocalizedText | null,
  phone: { src, width, height, alt: LocalizedText },     // the Mix card picture
  still: { src, width, height } | null,                  // desktop capture for the sheet; null for Nedaa
  loops: { desktop, mobile } | null,                     // DeckVideo paths; Bab and ElsewhereCo only
  embeds: { hero?: Embed, table?: Embed },               // SOLitaire only
  live: { href, label: LocalizedText } | null,
  downloads: { href, label: LocalizedText }[],
  casePath: string | null,                               // localised; null for Saqroon
  note: LocalizedText | null,                            // Saqroon's install note
  phase: { text, updated } | null,                       // English, from now.json, shown under the title
}
Embed      { src, title: LocalizedText }
HomeCardRef { slug, title, status, hasDownload }
```

Counts are computed from the list, never typed into copy. The order comes from `home.ts`. A test fails if a
project in `portfolio.ts` has no card, no aliases in all three scripts, or no answer.

### The fold

The fold is server-rendered HTML in normal page flow. It may be taller than the viewport on a phone, and it
is never pinned or clipped (finding M3). Order on a phone:

1.  name and role;
2.  the headline (`h1`);
3.  the résumé and WhatsApp buttons;
4.  the question box;
5.  the other leaf, with the headline in the other two languages.

The eight cards on the felt are decoration. They are plain `aria-hidden` images, not links and not focusable,
and they are the first thing cut on phones if space runs out.

The résumé and WhatsApp buttons are `TrackedAnchor`s that fire `resume_download` and `whatsapp_click` with
`placement: first-screen`. The WhatsApp prefill is localised.

### The question box

The server renders the box's markup: five chips, a text input that stays hidden until script runs, and an
empty answer area. `AskBox` is a client island that adds behaviour to that markup and does not render it.

**Without script:**

*   Three chips are plain links to the matching answers in the thread (`#q-live`, `#q-install`, `#q-services`).
*   The "Try the agent" and "Play SOLitaire" chips link to those two case pages.

**With script:**

*   The two sheet chips open their sheets (`[data-sheet]`, handled by `WorkTable`).
*   A chip or typed text is matched, and the matching answer takes the chips' place until "Ask another" (see
    Implementation Plan step 2.5).

**Answers.** Every answer is rendered on the server, in the page's language, by `AnswerView`, and placed in the
thread (see "The thread"). There are about 11 topic answers and one per project. The box shows an answer by
copying that server-rendered element into its answer area.

**What ships to the browser.** Only the matcher: the page locale's patterns, aliases and intent ids, passed
to `AskBox` as props. The answer text and the card data are not sent as script (finding M6). The only answer
built in the browser is the unknown answer, because its WhatsApp link carries the typed text.

**The rules** are a data table in `home.ts`: intent, patterns per locale, answer per locale, items.

*   Intents: live, install, services, process, résumé, stack, location, contact, price, play, demo, one per
    project, and unknown.
*   Price gets a plain answer: prices are quoted per project, with WhatsApp to ask.

**Matching.** Matching normalises the visitor's text with `normalize` from `src/lib/agent-demo.ts`. Patterns
and aliases go through the same `normalize` before they are compiled.

*   Words and project names match only as whole words. Arabic and Hebrew may carry a prefix attached to the
    word: ال, و, ب, ل, ف in Arabic; ה, ו, ב, ל, מ, ש, כ in Hebrew. So باب (Bab) matches بالباب but not
    أسباب, and سعر matches السعر.
*   Project names come from declared aliases in three scripts, never from slug words.
*   Bare channel names (WhatsApp, Telegram) are never project aliases. "Can you build a WhatsApp bot?" is a
    services question.

**Language.** Answers are always in the page's language. The four-field line still reports the language the
question was typed in (from `detectLanguage`), so a visitor who types English on `/ar` sees that it was
understood.

**Showing an answer.**

*   The answer replaces the chips, using the same `AnswerView` markup as the thread.
*   Under it are the four-field line, then "See it" when the answer has items.
*   Sources are named in visitor words from the glossary in `home.ts`, never as file names (finding M9).
*   Focus moves to the answer's heading, and nothing scrolls.
*   "Ask another" brings the chips back and returns focus to the first chip.

**Unknown.** The unknown answer shows what can be asked, and a `TrackedAnchor` to `wa.me`. The anchor's
prefill is the visitor's own text, URL-encoded and capped by the input's `maxlength` (280). It fires
`whatsapp_click` with `placement: answer`.

**Telemetry.** One `ask` event per answer, with `intent` and `source` (`chip` or `typed`). The Pro plan
keeps two fields per event, so `locale` is left out. Never the text.
Never the text.

### The zoom through the ف

**Today's zoom, as a visitor sees it.** Below the hero, the site's monogram fills the screen. As the visitor
scrolls, the page holds still, the opening in the monogram's ف grows until it fills the screen, and a project
screenshot shows through it. Then the page scrolls on.

*   The **stage** is the section that holds still while this plays (pinned).
*   The **opening** is the ف's counter, cut out of the monogram as a mask.
*   The **scene** is the picture seen through the opening.

`PortalMotion` drives this from the `data-portal-*` markup in `brand-portal.tsx`.

**`HomePortal`** is a new server component. It renders the eight hooks `PortalMotion` requires:

| Hook | What fills it |
| --- | --- |
| `[data-portal-stage]` | The section, `id="portal"`. |
| `[data-portal-poster]` | The monogram. |
| `[data-portal-mask]` | The ف's opening, from `@/lib/monogram` (`monogramPath`, `monogramCounter`). |
| `[data-portal-mark]` | The monogram's outline. |
| `[data-portal-copy]` | The project's title, as a `p`. There is no `h1` here, because the fold holds the page's only `h1`. |
| `[data-portal-actions]` | "Read the case study" for the project in view. |
| `[data-portal-photo]` | The scene picture, rendered by `PortalPicture`. It loads eagerly (`priority`). |
| `[data-portal-caption]` | The project's category and status. |

The section sits after the fold. Before any question, the scene shows the first card's screenshot.

**"See it"** is an `<a href="#portal">` that also dispatches a `home:see` DOM event with the first item's
slug. `PortalPicture` handles the swap:

1.  It preloads the new picture with `new Image()` and `decode()`.
2.  It sets `src` on the same `<img>` element. The element is never remounted and never given a React `key`,
    because `PortalMotion` holds a reference to it.
3.  The browser jumps to `#portal`, and `PortalMotion`'s existing hash and focus handling apply.

`PortalMotion` is not changed (finding M4). Because the new picture is decoded before the swap, the scene is
never in a half-loaded state.

`PortalMotion` keeps all its guards:

*   reduced motion;
*   a picture that has not loaded;
*   short viewports (less than 480 px below the header);
*   URL-bar resizes;
*   deep links to a hash;
*   keyboard focus inside the actions and caption;
*   restoring the page from the back-forward cache;
*   a hidden tab.

**`zoom_play`** fires once per picture:

*   when motion runs, as soon as `--portal-settle` on the stage falls to 0.5 or below (the opening is half
    open);
*   when motion is off (reduced motion or a short viewport), on arrival.

It carries `trigger: tap` if "See it" set the picture, and `scroll` otherwise.

### The work table

The server renders `#work` as twelve face-up cards in the Mix look. Each card is an `<article>`:

*   **The title link.** Its `::after` covers the whole card. It goes to the case page (`/work/<slug>`,
    localised). Saqroon's goes to its website, so without script, from a search engine, or with a
    modifier-click, every card has a real target.
*   **A visible row of links** under the title: the downloads and the live link. They are siblings of the title
    link, never inside it, and they sit above its `::after` layer, so each stays clickable.
*   **The picture.** The name is set large on a paper, ink or lime background (cycling by card index modulo 3),
    over the phone screenshot.
*   **The phase line** under the title, for the 8 cards with a `now.json` entry (E1).

Saqroon's card carries `id="saqroon"`. No card carries `id="agent"` (E3).

`WorkTable` is a client island that adds behaviour to the server-rendered cards through `data-*`
attributes. This follows the pattern of `WorkFilters` (`src/components/work-filters.tsx`). It does not render
the cards.

*   **The deal** (choice X12, owner, 2026-11-15):
    *   If `#work` is off screen when the page loads, the cards turn face down while out of view, so no
        visitor sees a flash.
    *   When about 30% of `#work` first becomes visible, the cards are dealt once, in `cardOrder`. Each card
        starts about 50 ms after the previous one, so the deal finishes in under 1 s. Each card's tilt is
        fixed by its index (`((index % 5) - 2) × 1.5°`), the same on the server and the client.
    *   There is no deal if the section is already on screen when the page loads, if it was reached through
        `/#work`, or under `prefers-reduced-motion`. The cards stay face up.
    *   Links stay clickable throughout.
*   **The calls** "All", "Live product" and "Install a preview" deal only the matching cards, and "Deal again"
    repeats the current call. Each fires `deal` with `call` (`all`, `live`, `install` or `again`).
    *   "Live product" means `status === "live-product"`.
    *   "Install a preview" means a card with at least one download.

### The project sheet

**How it works (choice A4, owner, 2026-11-15).** The sheet is a native `<dialog>` that `WorkTable` owns. One
instance serves every card. While it is open, the address bar shows the project's case-page address.

### When script breaks

**Each island owns only what it renders.** The server renders the chips, the cards, the answers, the thread
and the portal markup. The client islands (`AskBox`, `WorkTable`, `PortalPicture`, and `ProjectSheet` inside
`WorkTable`) only add behaviour to that markup. Each island renders only its own parts:

*   the answer area;
*   the dialog;
*   the deal state.

**The boundary.** Each island is wrapped in `IslandBoundary`, a plain React class error boundary. Its fallback
is `null`, so a crashed island loses only its added behaviour. The headline, both buttons, the chips as links,
the cards as links and the thread all stay usable.

*   The boundary calls `track("island_error", { island })` once, so a failure shows up in analytics instead of
    passing silently.
*   `HomePortal`'s client parts are wrapped the same way.

**What boundaries do not catch.** Errors thrown inside event handlers. Handlers that call `animate()`,
`showModal()` or `history` catch their own errors and fall back to the plain link's default action.

### Alt-A: An AI model behind the question box

*   **Pros**: Answers any phrasing in any language, with no rules table to keep.
*   **Cons**:
    *   costs money per visitor;
    *   can invent a price, a client or a feature;
    *   needs a server endpoint, a key and abuse limits;
    *   slower to answer.
*   **Reason for rejection**: The no-invented-proof rule matters more than coverage. A missed question already
    turns into a prefilled WhatsApp message, which is a lead.

### Alt-B: Port preview G or H directly

*   **Pros**: Fastest path to a page that looks like the preview.
*   **Cons**:
    *   The preview builds its content with script, so nothing shows without script (finding B4).
    *   Its canvas zoom lacks the real-phone guards `PortalMotion` has (finding M4).
    *   It carries a second, hand-copied data set that had already drifted from `portfolio.ts` (finding M7).
    *   It matches words with `\b`, which fails in Arabic and Hebrew (finding M1).
*   **Reason for rejection**: All four of those problems would ship with it. The preview stays as the visual
    reference only.

### Alt-C: Free text as the main path of the question box

*   **Pros**: Shows the same free-text interaction the agents offer to clients.
*   **Cons**:
    *   The most likely reply to free text is "I can't answer that" (finding M1).
    *   The rules need a test fixture per phrasing in three languages.
*   **Reason for rejection**: Localised suggested questions carry the box. Typed text is matched second and falls
    back to the WhatsApp offer.

### Alt-D: Services and process only inside answers

*   **Pros**: A shorter page, with the box doing more of the talking.
*   **Cons**:
    *   Without script, and for a visitor who never asks, the text is absent (finding B4).
    *   It is the only place on the homepage that names Kubernetes, Terraform and ArgoCD, which hirers scan for
        (finding B3).
    *   Two header links lose their targets.
*   **Reason for rejection**: The thread keeps the text visible, with the ids `services` and `process`.

### Alt-E: Automatic scroll through the zoom after an answer, and a four-step rail

*   **Pros**: Shows a message's full route as motion.
*   **Cons** (as built in preview G):
    *   It moves the visitor away from the answer and the first-screen buttons while the keyboard is open.
    *   Scrolling back erases the answer.
    *   One question writes into ten display elements across five sections (finding M5).
*   **Reason for rejection**: The zoom plays on "See it" or the visitor's own scroll. One four-field line sits
    under the answer.

### Alt-F: "I've passed it on" with a server send

*   **Pros**: The visitor's question reaches the owner without another tap.
*   **Cons**:
    *   As drafted it was false, since nothing was sent (finding B5).
    *   Making it true adds an email path with no protection (`src/app/actions/contact.ts` has no rate limit).
*   **Reason for rejection**: The page offers WhatsApp, prefilled with the visitor's words.

### Alt-G: Rewrite `portfolio-home.tsx` in place

*   **Pros**: No dead files left behind for a while.
*   **Cons**:
    *   No clean rollback.
    *   A long branch that rewrites shared content files will conflict with the Codex sessions, which commit
        content (project captures, APKs, Now lines) to `main` (finding M11).
*   **Reason for rejection**: New files, one cut-over commit, old files deleted later.

### Alt-H: Launch in slices

*   **Pros**: The staging proposed in concepts_brief.md. Each part (first screen, cards, answer list, free text)
    meets real visitors sooner.
*   **Cons**:
    *   Four cut-overs instead of one.
    *   For weeks the homepage would be half the old design and half the new.
    *   Rollback becomes per slice.
*   **Reason for rejection**: The owner chose one cut-over on 2026-11-11, because a half-old, half-new homepage
    is worse for both audiences than a short wait.

The sheet's address (A1, A2, A3), the unmirrored fold (B2), dropping "Instagram" (C2) and a homepage-only font
(D1) are recorded once, under "Owner choices".

*   **Rollback.** Revert the cut-over commit, or promote the production deployment from just before the cut-over
    in Vercel (record its hash when the cut-over is made).
    *   Reverting restores today's page, including the font.

        *   `normalize` is only exported.

        *   `LiveEmbed` gains an unused optional prop.
        *   `AgentDemo` fires one more event.
        *   The footer markup is identical.
*   **Script failure.** See "When script breaks".

#### 1.4 `src/lib/home-ask.ts` (Create)

Pure TypeScript.

**Server-only exports:**

*   `answersFor(locale): Answer[]`. Each `Answer` is `{ id, intent, text, items: HomeCardRef[], handoff,
    sourceLabel, whatsappHref | null }`.
    *   `text` is in the page's language.
    *   `id` is `q-<intent>`, or `q-<slug>` for a project intent.
*   `matcherFor(locale): Matcher`. The compiled pattern sources for that locale's intents and aliases, as
    strings, so they can be passed to the client as props.

**Shared export, for the server and the browser:**

*   `matchIntent(text, matcher): string | null`.
    *   Empty or whitespace-only input returns `null`.
    *   Matching runs on `normalize(text)`. Every pattern and alias went through `normalize` before it was
        escaped and compiled into `(?<!\p{L})(?:PREFIX)?<escaped>(?!\p{L})` with the `iu` flags.
    *   `PREFIX` is `ال|وال|بال|لل|و|ب|ل|ف` for Arabic patterns and `ה|ו|ב|ל|מ|ש|כ` for Hebrew ones. Latin
        patterns have none.
    *   Rules are checked in a fixed order: `services` first (so "build" plus a channel word wins), then
        project aliases, then résumé, stack, play, demo, install, live, process, location, contact, price.
    *   No match returns `"unknown"`.
*   `unknownWhatsappHref(text)`. Returns the `wa.me` base taken from `profile.contactLinks` (the WhatsApp
    entry), with `?text=` and `encodeURIComponent(text)` appended.

**Language.** `detectLanguage(text)` fills the four-field line only. Answers are always in the page's
language.

**Props:** the `Matcher` for the page locale, the answer ids, `labels`, and the page `locale`. It renders no

**After hydration:**

*   The three answer chips become buttons (`role="button"`, `href` kept for middle-click).
*   The text input and send button appear.
*   The two sheet chips keep their `data-sheet` attribute and are handled by `WorkTable`.

**On an answer chip or a send:**

1.  The chip's intent, or `matchIntent(text, matcher)`, picks an answer id.
2.  The box clones the server-rendered answer element (`#q-…`) into the answer area **in place of the chips**
